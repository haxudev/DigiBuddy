# Risk tiers: how high to set the gate

## Why tiers exist

One approval pattern is either excessive for a reversible draft or dangerously
weak for a payment. Risk changes the depth and placement of review, never the
requirement that intent, capabilities, and a plan exist before execution.

Take the highest tier reached by any signal.

| Tier | Signals | Review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Low** | reversible, minutes of work, no external effect, draft for requester only | eligible for one Compact review when every Compact condition holds |
| **Medium** | several steps, meaningful time/cost, shared output, or difficult reversal | Full review of brief and plan before authorization |
| **High** | irreversible/external action, money, legal exposure, personal data, or data loss | Full review plus an action gate immediately before each high-risk effect |

## Signals

**Reversibility.** Can another person undo it in under an hour? Sending,
publishing, paying, filing, or deleting is not reversible. Drafting locally is.

**Blast radius.** A private draft is lower risk than a board slide or customer
message, even if both are files.

**Cost.** Real budget, scarce quota, or over roughly half an hour of committed
waiting is at least medium.

**Exposure.** Credentials, personal data, regulated material, and contractual
content raise the tier. Do not solicit credentials; ask the user to authenticate
or provide a redacted export.

## Useful action gates

A gate states what will happen, cost and duration, what cannot be undone, and
the viable alternatives. Recommend one only when evidence supports it; otherwise
state that there is no evidence-based preference. "Shall I proceed?" without
context is not informed approval.

Low-risk plans need no extra action gate after execution authorization. Medium
and high plans place gates immediately before the risk they protect, not at the
end after the action already happened.

## Risk changes

Re-evaluate when facts change. If Compact work discovers external effects,
sensitive data, a capability gap, a mid-execution decision, a known replanning
branch, or work that no longer fits one session, stop and upgrade to Full before
continuing. Step count alone is not an escalation signal. Escalation is safe;
silent de-escalation is not.

No tier permits retrospective approval or an unchecked completion claim.
Verification and chronology are evidence rules, not optional ceremony.
