# Profile: competitive teardown

- **Profile id:** competitor-teardown
- **Base profile:** none

## Dimensions to clarify

| Dimension | Deferrable | Why it decides the shape of the work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **[market-category] Market and category** | no | Defined too broadly, the sweep returns dozens of irrelevant brands and the comparison becomes a directory. "Furniture" is not a market; "mid-price UK direct-to-consumer sofas" is. |
| **[competitor-boundary] Competitor boundary** | no | The requester almost always has two or three names in mind that must be covered. Discovering them at review time means redoing the collection. Ask which are mandatory, and which adjacent players are deliberately excluded. |
| **[business-model-split] Business model split** | no | Direct-to-consumer upstarts and established retailers are not comparable on the same axes. Pricing, assortment depth, and channel mix mean different things in each. Decide which population is in scope. |
| **[report-audience] Report audience** | no | A design team wants positioning, visual language, and product detail. An investor wants unit economics, growth signals, and market structure. A buyer wants a scored matrix. The same evidence produces three different documents. |
| **[channel-scope] Channel scope** | no | Company sites and marketplace listings are open. Social platforms and analyst material usually need a session or a subscription. Settle up front which channels count, because an unreachable channel is a disclosed hole in the coverage, not a finding about the competitor. |
| **[depth-per-competitor] Depth per competitor** | no | Comparing four competitors on twelve attributes is a different task from comparing twelve on four. Fix the shape before collecting anything. |
| **[recency-window] Recency window** | no | Pricing and assortment go stale fast; positioning does not. State the window, because a comparison mixing this month's prices with last year's range reads as authoritative and is not. |

## Step skeleton

1. **[step-fix-frame] Fix the comparison frame.** The attribute set every competitor will be scored on, agreed before collection. Deciding attributes after seeing the data is how the frame ends up fitted to whichever competitor was easiest to research.
2. **[step-assemble-set] Assemble the competitor set.** Mandatory names from the requester, plus discovered ones if in scope. Have the list confirmed before spending collection effort on it.
3. **[step-collect-uniformly] Collect per competitor.** One artifact per competitor, each attribute carrying the source it came from. Uniform structure now is what makes the matrix possible later.
4. **[step-normalise] Normalise.** Currencies, units, tiers, and pack sizes into a single basis. This is where most comparisons quietly break: two prices that are not the same kind of price.
5. **[step-score-rank] Score and rank** against the agreed frame, with weights the requester set rather than ones you chose.
6. **[step-draw-conclusions] Draw conclusions,** each one pointing at the evidence that supports it.
7. **[step-state-coverage] State the coverage** — which competitors, which attributes, which channels, over what window — and what the holes do to the conclusions.

## Acceptance criteria

- **[criterion-mandatory-set]** Every mandatory competitor named by the requester appears in the final comparison, or its absence is explained.
- **[criterion-explicit-cells]** Every cell in the matrix is either filled with a sourced value or explicitly marked not-published, unreachable, or not-applicable. Blank cells are defects; they read as zero.
- **[criterion-price-basis]** Prices are on one basis, and the basis is stated.
- **[criterion-sourced-claims]** Every claim about a competitor cites where it came from.
- **[criterion-fixed-attributes]** Attributes were fixed before collection, not fitted to it afterwards.
- **[criterion-recency-window]** The recency window is stated and consistent.
- **[criterion-channel-gaps]** Channels that could not be reached are listed with what that leaves unknown.

## Known pitfalls

**[pitfall-unreachable-is-empty] Treating an unreachable channel as an empty one.** A competitor whose social presence could not be read is a competitor whose social presence is unknown. Writing "no social activity" turns a gap in your access into a false claim about their business, and it is the single most damaging error in this domain because it reads as a finding.

**[pitfall-price-basis] Comparing list prices to promoted prices.** Discounting cadence differs by business model. Capture both, or state which one you used everywhere.

**[pitfall-easy-frame] Letting the easiest competitor set the frame.** Whichever company publishes the most detail will pull the attribute list toward its own strengths. Fixing attributes first is the defence.

**[pitfall-copy-is-operations] Confusing the marketing site with the business.** Positioning copy describes intent. Assortment, pricing, stock depth, and delivery terms describe operations. Do not report the first as the second.

**[pitfall-recency-drift] Silent recency drift.** Pages captured across several weeks of collection, presented as a snapshot. Record when each was captured.

**[pitfall-over-collecting] Over-collecting.** Twelve competitors at shallow depth rarely beats four at real depth, and it costs far more. Depth is usually what makes the comparison decision-grade.

**[pitfall-unweighted-score] Scoring without weights.** An unweighted matrix implies every attribute matters equally, which is never true. Get the weights from whoever is making the decision.
